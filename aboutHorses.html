About

This is a site for sorting Japanese thoroughbred racehorses 🐎

Want to find horses based on simple attributes like the color of their coat, their career status, or their gender? Well then this is the place for you! Whether you're new to horse racing or already know a thing or two, this site will help you find cute horsies to learn more about ^_^

Terms

(for the uninitiated)

  • JRA: "The Japan Racing Association (Japanese: 日本中央競馬会 Nippon Chūō Keiba Kai, or JRA) is a public company established in Japan under a law to operate Chūō Keiba (中央競馬 Central horse racing) and to manage racecourses, betting facilities, and horse-training facilities in the country" ⤴
  • Grade Stakes/Group Races: "Group races, also known as Pattern races, or Graded races in some jurisdictions, are the highest level of races in Thoroughbred horse racing." ⤴
  • Grade 1 Races: "The highest level, for races with major international importance considered to be of championship standard" ⤴
  • Grade 2 Races: "Races that have significant international importance, but are below a championship standard." ⤴
  • Grade 3 Races: "Other races of note, mainly of national rather than international importance." ⤴
  • Starting Gate: "A machine used to ensure a fair start to in horse racing" ⤴
  • Coat: Color of the horses' fur coat
  • Foaled: When the horse was born
  • Genders: There are mutliple terms for a horse's gender depending on age.
  • Colt/Filly: Male/female horses that are 3 years old and under, respectively
  • Horse/Mare: Male/female horses that are 4 years and older
  • Gelding: A neutered male horse
  • Broodmare: Female horses kept for breeding
  • Stud: Male horses kept for breeding
  • Off-Track Thoroughbred (OTTB): "A thoroughbred horse who has raced or trained to race and has now transitioned to a career beyond racing." ⤴
  • Surface: Horse races can take place on various surfaces, which require different strengths to run well on.
  • Some horses have the ability to run on multiple surfaces, or transitioned from one surface to another.
  • Most tracks use natural surfaces such as dirt or turf, but there are some man-made track surfaces out there.
